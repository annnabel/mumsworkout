## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-crafted, caring piece of product design. The visual system is disciplined and the injury-prevention content architecture — Setup / Do this / Avoid blocks, the effort meter standing in for RPE, breathing cues, an always-reachable safety page — is the best thing here. It clearly serves its one job.

The gaps are not visual; they are in the **flow's robustness**. The single biggest opportunity: this is a phone-used-at-the-gym app for someone who will be interrupted constantly (rest between sets, a text, the screen locking), yet the workout keeps its position in a plain in-memory variable with no persistence, no resume, and no history-back integration. The prettiest exercise screen doesn't help if she loses her place at set 3 and lands back on Home.

## What's Working

- **The safety + injury-prevention system.** Colour-coded Set up / Do this / Avoid blocks, the plain-word effort meter replacing any numeric scale, per-exercise breathing cues, and a dedicated "when to stop and see a professional" page with a correctly-rare use of red. The content genuinely reduces injury risk, and the visual system makes the cues impossible to miss.
- **Accessibility-led sizing and states.** 18px root, ≥60px tap targets, `ink-soft` held at ~4.5:1, real `:focus-visible` outlines, `aria-pressed` / `aria-expanded`, and the graceful labelled placeholder that names the exact missing GIF filename. Live screenshots confirm even "Prone Leg Curl Machine" wraps without overflow at 320px.
- **Bilingual EN/VI with persistence.** A real, specific act of care for the actual user, not a generic i18n bolt-on.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Workout progress is not persisted — one interruption sends her back to step 1
`flowIndex` (app.js:993) is an in-memory variable. There is no save-on-advance and no resume. On a ~50-minute gym session the phone will lock repeatedly and mobile browsers aggressively evict backgrounded tabs; any reload, tab eviction, or return-after-a-break resets the flow to Home with no way back to where she was.
- **Why it matters:** This is the app's real-world failure mode for its exact persona. Losing her place mid-workout is disorienting and, for a non-technical 50+ user, likely to end the session.
- **Fix:** Persist `{ flowIndex, dateKey }` to `localStorage` on every step change (the store helpers already exist). On load, if there's an in-progress workout from today, offer a calm "Resume where you left off / Start over" choice on Home.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Browser back / Android gesture-back exits the app instead of stepping back
The router never touches `history` (app.js:996–1017). Pressing the hardware/gesture Back button — deeply ingrained on Android — closes the site or leaves for the previous page rather than going to the previous exercise.
- **Why it matters:** A direct User-Control failure. The in-app back arrow only appears from step 2 onward, and the platform back gesture actively does the wrong thing, which is exactly what a non-technical user reaches for.
- **Fix:** Wire routing to `history.pushState` / `popstate` so Back = previous step and never traps or unexpectedly ejects. Pair with the × exit getting a lightweight "Leave workout? Your place is saved" confirm once persistence exists.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Focus is lost on every view change (keyboard / screen-reader users lose their place)
Each navigation replaces `#app.innerHTML` (app.js:1006–1013); focus drops to `<body>`. A keyboard or screen-reader user has to tab from the top of the document after every Next.
- **Why it matters:** Undermines the otherwise strong accessibility posture for exactly the users who most need it.
- **Fix:** After render, move focus to the new view's heading (`tabindex="-1"` + `.focus()`), and reconsider `aria-live="polite"` on the whole `#app` — it re-announces the entire screen on each swap. A targeted status region for step changes is calmer.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Ephemeral checklist / accordion state resets on re-render and language switch
Checklist ticks and accordion open/closed are pure class toggles (app.js:1427–1440), never stored. Switching language mid-flow calls `render(currentRoute)` and silently wipes any cool-down items she'd ticked.
- **Why it matters:** Small but erodes trust — an action she took visibly undoes itself.
- **Fix:** Persist checklist state (or at least preserve it across a same-view language re-render); keep accordion state per exercise.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P3] The "vitals" value renders variable-length strings at a fixed 1.9rem
`.vbig` is tuned for short values like "2 sets", but warm-up doses flow through the same slot: "10 each leg" becomes three oversized lines and unbalances the two side-by-side cards.
- **Why it matters:** Cosmetic, but it reads as slightly unfinished against an otherwise precise system.
- **Fix:** Give the dose vital a smaller step (or `clamp()` the value size / allow a two-line compact treatment) so long doses sit as neatly as "2 sets".
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)** — the primary persona:
- Locks the phone during a between-set rest; returns to find the workout reset to Home (no state persistence). *Most damaging red flag.*
- Reaches for the Android back gesture out of habit → leaves the app instead of going back a step.
- Otherwise well-served: primary actions sit in the thumb zone (sticky footer), tap targets are large, one idea per screen.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**:
- Focus lands on `<body>` after every Next; must re-tab from the top of each exercise.
- `aria-live="polite"` on the entire `#app` announces the whole new screen on each navigation — verbose and fatiguing.
- Strong foundation otherwise: visible focus rings, labelled controls, contrast holds, effort conveyed in words not colour alone.

**Linh, 58 — not comfortable with technology (project-specific persona):**
- Expects the phone Back button to mean "go back one screen"; instead it exits — confusing and slightly alarming.
- An accidental tap on the × abandons the whole workout with no "are you sure" and no saved place.
- Very well-served by the plain language, oversized type, and the complete absence of hidden menus or gestures.

## Minor Observations

- The `Demo: <source>` credit sits over the bottom of the GIF; on the marching-high-knees frame it overlaps the figure's feet. Consider seating it just below the frame, or on a stronger scrim.
- No rest guidance between sets and no timer — likely a deliberate scope choice, but worth a conscious decision for a strength program.
- Linear flow gives no quick jump to a specific exercise inside the workout; fine for first-time use, mildly tedious for a returning user (the plan page covers browsing).
- `todayKey()` uses local date parts — correct for a personal single-user tool; just noting it's timezone-local by design.
